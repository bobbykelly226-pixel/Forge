# Category 10 authoritative catalog excerpt

Authoritative Category 10 content after the 10 question reduction.

## Category 10: Integrity, Honesty & Trust

### 1. Which behaviors most strongly build trust for you?

**Format:** Select up to five

Telling the truth even when it may create discomfort
Following through on promises and commitments
Being consistent across words, actions, and circumstances
Voluntarily sharing information that materially affects the relationship
Acknowledging mistakes without being forced to admit them
Correcting inaccurate information promptly
Communicating when a commitment can no longer be kept
Respecting agreed upon relationship boundaries
Protecting private information shared in confidence
Making important decisions openly
Behaving consistently when a partner is not present
Defending the relationship against inappropriate outside interference
Being dependable during stressful or inconvenient circumstances
Allowing questions without becoming evasive or retaliatory
Demonstrating changed behavior after causing harm

**Priority follow up:** Of the behaviors you selected, which two are most essential for establishing lasting trust?

**Alignment purpose:** Identifies the concrete behaviors through which the respondent recognizes integrity rather than relying on a broad self description.

### 2. Which information do you believe partners generally have a responsibility to disclose?

**Format:** Select all that apply

**Implementation note:** This is an unrestricted multi select. “Nothing beyond what each partner voluntarily chooses to disclose” is mutually exclusive with all specific disclosure responsibilities.

“Disclosure responsibilities depend on the relationship stage and the information’s practical effect” may coexist with specific selections and should be stored as a contextual qualification.

Existing marriage, engagement, domestic partnership, or committed relationship
Sexual or romantic involvement that affects exclusivity agreements
Children, dependents, or substantial caregiving responsibilities
Major debt, financial obligations, or financial decisions affecting a shared future
Serious health information that materially affects the relationship or informed consent
A significant legal matter that could affect safety, finances, housing, employment, or family life
Substance use or another behavior that materially affects reliability, safety, or shared plans
Ongoing contact with a former partner when it materially affects the relationship
A major conflict of interest or divided loyalty
A substantial change in relationship intentions
A serious breach of an agreed upon boundary
Information showing that an important prior statement was materially inaccurate
A major personal commitment that would affect time, location, finances, or household responsibilities
Nothing beyond what each partner voluntarily chooses to disclose (mutually exclusive)
Disclosure responsibilities depend on the relationship stage and the information’s practical effect

**Alignment purpose:** Identifies what the respondent believes informed commitment requires without demanding irrelevant, unsafe, or indiscriminate disclosure.

### 3. How should personal privacy function within a committed relationship?

**Format:** Privacy boundary range

**Context note:** Privacy is not automatically secrecy. At the same time, labeling something private does not excuse concealing information that materially affects a partner’s safety, consent, finances, or relationship decisions.

**Implementation note:** “No fixed approach” should be stored separately from the ordered privacy boundary range.

Partners should have very little private information because commitment requires broad openness
Privacy is appropriate for minor matters, but most personal information should remain accessible
Each person may retain private thoughts, conversations, belongings, and personal space unless they affect the relationship
Privacy should be respected unless there is a specific and reasonable concern about harm or deception
Partners should maintain substantial personal privacy even within a deeply committed relationship
Each person should control access to their devices, accounts, communications, and private information
No fixed approach. The boundary should depend on the subject and the couple’s agreements

**Alignment purpose:** Measures expectations concerning autonomy, access, confidentiality, reassurance, and the distinction between privacy and concealment.

### 4. If telling the full truth would cause immediate hurt or conflict, which principle should most strongly guide the response?

**Format:** Scenario based choice

**Context note:** The selected answer identifies the principle that should carry the greatest influence. It does not establish that every situation should be handled identically.

Tell the truth directly because discomfort does not justify deception
Tell the truth compassionately and choose an appropriate time and setting
Share the material truth while avoiding unnecessary or intentionally hurtful detail
Pause briefly to understand the situation before speaking, but do not create a false account
Seek advice or support before disclosing when the situation is especially sensitive
Delay disclosure only when immediate safety, medical stability, or another serious concern requires it
Respect privacy when the information does not materially affect the partner or relationship
Recognize that some situations require judgment rather than one universal rule

**Alignment purpose:** Examines how the respondent balances honesty, compassion, timing, relevance, privacy, and avoidance of unnecessary harm.

### 5. When someone realizes they gave their partner inaccurate or incomplete information, what should they primarily do?

**Format:** Scenario based choice

Correct it immediately and explain exactly what was inaccurate
Correct it as soon as reasonably possible and provide the necessary context
Disclose the correction when the information materially affects the partner or relationship
Take time to understand why it happened before discussing it
Seek help preparing for the conversation when disclosure may have serious consequences
Correct the information and accept that trust may take time to rebuild
Focus on changed behavior when repeated explanations would not restore trust
Use judgment when the inaccuracy was minor and has no meaningful effect

**Alignment purpose:** Measures expectations for voluntary correction, timeliness, context, accountability, and behavioral repair.

### 6. If a partner repeatedly fails to keep commitments, which principle should most strongly guide the response?

**Format:** Scenario based choice

**Context note:** Repeated failure may reflect dishonesty, unrealistic expectations, executive function challenges, illness, disability, changing circumstances, or another cause. Forge should not infer motive from the pattern alone.

Reliability is essential, and repeated broken commitments should be treated as a serious trust issue
The couple should identify whether the problem involves capacity, organization, avoidance, or dishonesty
Commitments should be reduced or revised so expectations become realistic
The partner should communicate earlier instead of repeatedly promising what they cannot deliver
Trust should depend on sustained improvement rather than additional promises
Outside support may be needed when the pattern continues despite sincere effort
Some missed commitments are manageable when they do not involve essential responsibilities
An unresolved pattern may reveal a fundamental incompatibility

**Alignment purpose:** Measures how the respondent evaluates dependability, capacity, communication, patterns, and behavioral evidence.

### 7. After a serious breach of trust, which principle should most strongly guide what happens next?

**Format:** Trust repair posture with a separate context dependent state

**Context note:** Forgiveness, reconciliation, continued participation in the relationship, and restored trust are separate decisions. None automatically requires the others.

**Implementation note:** “The appropriate response depends…” is a separate context dependent state and should not be treated as a position on an ordered scale.

The remaining six responses should be stored as distinct trust repair postures rather than ranked from more forgiving to less forgiving.

Trust may be rebuilt when there is full accountability, appropriate transparency, and sustained change
Trust may be rebuilt gradually through agreed boundaries and consistent behavior
The harmed partner should determine whether repair is possible and set its pace
Reconciliation may be attempted without assuming trust will fully return
Forgiveness may be possible even when the relationship cannot continue
Some breaches permanently end the possibility of a safe or healthy relationship
The appropriate response depends on the nature, severity, pattern, and consequences of the breach

**Alignment purpose:** Identifies the principle the respondent believes should carry the greatest influence after a serious breach while preserving the right to safety, boundaries, separation, and independent judgment.

### 8. When a partner asks for proof or reassurance because trust has been damaged, what approach feels most appropriate?

**Format:** Reassurance and access range

**Context note:** Reassurance must remain voluntary, proportionate, and time limited when possible. A breach of trust does not grant permanent ownership of another person’s devices, accounts, location, communications, or identity.

**Implementation note:** “No fixed approach” should be stored separately from the ordered reassurance and access range.

Broad temporary transparency is reasonable when someone has seriously damaged trust
Specific, mutually agreed verification may be appropriate for a limited period
Reassurance should focus mainly on communication and consistent behavior
Requests for access should be evaluated individually according to relevance and proportionality
Device, account, location, or message access should not be required even during trust repair
If trust requires ongoing monitoring, the relationship may no longer be sustainable
No fixed approach. The response should depend on safety, consent, severity, and the repair agreement

**Alignment purpose:** Measures expectations concerning reassurance, verification, autonomy, privacy, proportionality, and the limits of surveillance.

### 9. When handling confidential information shared by a partner, which principle should carry the greatest influence?

**Format:** Scenario based choice

**Context note:** Several principles may matter simultaneously. The selected answer identifies the principle that should carry the greatest influence when confidentiality, support seeking, safety, and other responsibilities compete.

Confidentiality does not require concealing abuse, imminent danger, exploitation, serious threats, or information that must legally or ethically be reported.

Keep it private unless the partner gives permission to share it
Share only the minimum necessary information when seeking trusted advice or professional support
Discuss in advance which people may be included in sensitive relationship matters
Protect privacy while allowing appropriate disclosure when safety or serious harm is involved
Respect legal, medical, professional, or caregiving responsibilities that may require disclosure
Never use private information to embarrass, threaten, control, or gain leverage
Consider the legitimate privacy rights of other people affected by the information
Use judgment based on consent, safety, necessity, and foreseeable harm

**Alignment purpose:** Measures the respondent’s leading expectation concerning loyalty, consent, discretion, support seeking, safety exceptions, and responsible handling of sensitive information.

### 10. Which integrity related behaviors would most seriously threaten long term compatibility?

**Format:** Select up to five

Repeated or deliberate lying about matters that affect the relationship
Concealing another committed relationship or violating exclusivity agreements
Hiding major financial, legal, health, substance related, or caregiving information that affects shared decisions
Maintaining secret communications or relationships that violate agreed boundaries
Manipulating facts to make me doubt my memory, perception, or judgment
Blaming others or refusing responsibility after causing harm
Repeatedly making commitments with no sincere effort to follow through
Using private information to shame, threaten, expose, or control me
Demanding unrestricted access to my devices, accounts, location, or communications
Monitoring, impersonating, tracking, or recording me without legitimate consent
Pressuring me to lie, conceal wrongdoing, or compromise an essential value
Retaliating when I ask reasonable questions or raise a concern
Presenting a substantially different identity, history, or intention in order to secure the relationship
Repeating serious behavior after apologizing and promising change
A breach of trust that makes informed consent, emotional safety, or a shared future impossible

**Priority follow up:** Of the behaviors you selected, which two would be most difficult or impossible for you to move past?

**Alignment purpose:** Identifies essential trust boundaries and high impact incompatibilities without assuming the respondent has experienced or engaged in the selected conduct.

### Locked product decisions

Former Q1 was removed because nearly every respondent is likely to rate honesty as important, which provides little useful differentiation.
Former Q3 was removed because the retained privacy boundary question captures openness and autonomy more precisely.
Former Q8 was removed because Category 4 owns apology, accountability, and repair after harm.
Former Q13 was removed because Category 6 owns parenting expectations.
Former Q14 was removed because the retained privacy, disclosure, reassurance, confidentiality, and serious incompatibility questions already define workable differences.
Multiselect questions are not fully ranked. Only Q1 and Q10 receive a lightweight “choose the two most important” follow up.
Category 10 must never display an honesty score, trust ranking, integrity percentage, moral judgment label, or safety diagnosis.
Private boundary answers must not be interpreted as evidence that the respondent has experienced or engaged in the selected behavior.
Written responses are excluded because this category has no defined use for them at launch.
Structured answers power alignment; follow up priorities determine added weight.
